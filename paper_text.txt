 A B S T R A C T
Agricultural research produces vast amounts of unstructured textual data, which remains largely underutilized 
due to the lack of robust tools for automated processing. If effectively processed, this underutilized data 
can provide critical insights to advance agricultural practices, decision-making, and sustainability. This work 
focuses on applying Named Entity Recognition (NER) and Relation Extraction (RE) to convert unstructured 
data into structured formats, addressing the challenges of domain-specific terminology and limited annotated 
datasets. This scarcity is primarily due to the domain-specific terminology, contextual complexity, and 
lack of annotated data in the agricultural domain. This study addresses these challenges by proposing 
sophisticated data augmentation techniques, validated using large language models and human reviewers, to 
enhance training data. We introduce AgNER-BERTa and AgRE-BERTa, two encoder-based models tailored for 
agricultural NER and RE tasks, and compare them with state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselines, including SciBERT, 
SpanBERT, and generative decoder models like ChatGPT. Our experiments demonstrate superior performance, 
achieving 98% accuracy for NER and 97% for RE outperforming SOTA models. The extracted entities and 
relations are used to construct the Agricultural Knowledge Graph (AgKG), providing structured, queryable 
insights to support precision agriculture, policy-making, and sustainable farming practices.
1. Introduction
In recent years, the thrust of research in agriculture has generated 
a large volume of information but in most cases, this information is 
unstructured (Fountas et al., 2024). This makes it extremely difficult 
and inefficient to extract and use the key information. It is practically 
impossible to analyze such volumes of data manually, which is very 
time-consuming and prone to human errors. To address this challenge, 
Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, particularly NER and 
RE, are increasingly being used to automate the identification of im-
portant entities and their relationships (Qiao et al., 2022). This process 
allows for the conversion of unstructured text into structured, machine-
readable formats, facilitating more effective data utilization. However, 
a major complication in implementing deep learning models for NER 
and RE in the agricultural domain is the lack of large annotated datasets 
that cover a large variety of entities. Textual data augmentation can 
address this challenge (Shorten, Khoshgoftaar, & Furht, 2021), but 
its implementation is challenging due to the inherent complexity of 
natural language and the presence of domain-specific terminology.

1.1. Motivation
With the increasing availability of agricultural research data which 
is mostly unstructured, there is a growing need for automated methods 
to extract meaningful insights efficiently. However, extracting mean-
ingful information from unstructured text is a complex task due to 
domain-specific terminology, contextual ambiguity, and the scarcity of 
annotated datasets. The AgriNER dataset, earlier proposed by us De, 
Sanyal, and Mukherjee (2023), contains thirty-six types of agricultural 
named entities and nine types of relations including symmetric and 
asymmetric relations, and sentences, extracted from agriculture re-
search papers. The labeled dataset, though rich with diverse entity and 
relation types, is quite limited in size. In this paper, we aim to enlarge 
it automatically using Large Language Models (LLMs). Deep learning 
models, which are increasingly being adopted with great success for 
various NLP tasks, require large amounts of training data. The scarcity 
of annotated datasets in agriculture often poses a significant challenge 
to applying deep neural models like LLMs in this domain. We believe 
that our augmented dataset will help mitigate this issue to a great 
extent.

1.2. Contributions
In this work, we develop and evaluate two encoder-based models,
AgNER-BERTa and AgRE-BERTa, specifically fine-tuned for agricul-
tural NER and RE tasks. As an attempt to mitigate the issue of data 
scarcity, we devise novel textual data augmentation techniques using 
Large Language Models (LLMs), validated by human reviewers and 
LLMs (Bedemariam et al., 2025), to enhance the AgriNER dataset. Our 
approach ensures the generation of high-quality synthetic data while 
preserving contextual integrity. Furthermore, we compare our models 
against state-of-the-art baselines, including SciBERT, SpanBERT, and 
generative decoder models such as ChatGPT1, showing that fine-tuned 
encoder-based models significantly outperform generative models in 
agricultural NER and RE tasks. Note that the use of encoder-based 
models for NER and RE avoids the problem of hallucination, unlike the 
scenario where LLMs are directly prompted to perform these tasks. The 
extracted entities and relationships are then structured into an Agri-
cultural Knowledge Graph (AgKG), providing a valuable resource for 
precision agriculture, policy-making, and sustainable farming practices. 
The code and the augmented data are publicly available on github2. The 
main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We present a novel textual data augmentation approach for NER 
and RE datasets utilizing LLMs, addressing the lack of annotated 
agriculture data.
• We develop and fine-tune two transformer (encoder) models,
AgNER-BERTa and AgRE-BERTa for the NER and RE tasks within 
the agriculture domain, outperforming several state-of-the-art 
models by a significant margin.
• We construct an Agricultural Knowledge Graph, AgKG, from 
the identified entities and their relations, providing structured 
and queryable agriculture knowledge for further research and 
decision-making.
The rest of our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we survey 
the literature on NER and RE in the agricultural domain. We introduce 
our dataset in Section 3. In Section 4, we describe the architecture of 
the proposed system for NER and RE. In particular, we discuss data 
augmentation in Section 4.1, the NER model in Section 4.2, the RE 
model in Section 4.3, and knowledge graph construction in Section 4.4. 
We present the experimental results in Section 5 and the analysis in 
Section 6. In Section 7, we attempt to determine which of the two types 
of models are more appropriate for the NER and RE tasks: encoder-
based models or decoder-based models. We present future directions in 
Section 8 and conclude in Section 9.
2. Literature survey
This section explores the existing research on NER and RE, specif-
ically in the agricultural domain. First, the importance of natural 
language processing in this domain is discussed, followed by a survey 
of the advancements of NER and RE tasks in agriculture.
2.1. Overview and importance of NLP in agriculture
NLP has emerged as a demanding technology in various sectors that 
revolutionize information processing. In agriculture, NLP is crucial in 
improving productivity, sustainability, and decision-making processes. 
Researchers can extract valuable information from the vast amount 
of textual data by applying NLP methods. In the agriculture domain, 
feedback from farmers, and vendors is much needed, and sentiment 
analysis can be used here to understand the trends and market improve-
ments (Sharma, Vora, Shaw, & Patil, 2024). NLP techniques can be

2.2. Advancements of NER and RE in agriculture
NER is a sub-task of Information Extraction (IR), that has been 
explored in various domains such as the Biomedical and Healthcare, Fi-
nance, Legal, News, Media, Government, Academic research, and many 
more. It is important to identify disease names, symptoms, drugs, treat-
ments, and medical procedures in the biomedical and healthcare do-
main so that the deep learning models can benefit the domain (Perera, 
Dehmer, & Emmert-Streib, 2020; Weber, Münchmeyer, Rocktäschel, 
Habibi, & Leser, 2020). In domains like the finance sector, NER plays a 
great role by recognizing financial entities such as companies, stocks, 
currencies, etc to extract information from financial reports, news, 
stock markets, etc (Jayakumar, Krishnakumar, Peddagopu, & Sridhar, 
2020). Extracting useful information from legal documents such as 
laws, rules and regulations, court cases, etc, NER is the solution to 
go with textual data as legal documents are mostly in text format. 
NER helps to extract that information and that can be used further 
for many use cases (Naik, Patel, & Kannan, 2023). We now discuss 
works pertinent to the agricultural domain. Malarkodi, Lex, and Devi 
(2016) have proposed a NER dataset in agriculture with nineteen types 
of tags and a Conditional Random Fields (CRFs) based approach to 
implement the NER model. Guo et al. (2020) proposed an annotated 
corpus that mainly focuses on Chinese agricultural diseases and pests. 
A Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) with CRF model 
is implemented for the NER task. Later they have used a pre-trained 
language model like BERT with a 3D CNN to improve the performance 
scores (Guo et al., 2022). Wang, Jiang and Liu (2023) have proposed 
a BiLSTM-CRF-based weakly supervised model to construct a knowl-
edge graph. Wu et al. (2024) proposed an improved version of the 
Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BI-GRU) neural network model 
for NER of rice genes and phenotypes. Veena, Kanjirangat, and Gupta 
(2023) proposed an unsupervised method using BERT with LDA topic 
modeling, covering only six entities. Though deep learning techniques 
are being applied to extract named entities in various domains, only 
a few works have been done in the agricultural field. Hence, a NER 
model capable of detecting a variety of agricultural named entities is 
required for further research.
RE methods extend the effectiveness of NER by linking the entities 
in meaningful ways, named as relations. In Zhou, Su, Zhang, and 
Zhang (2005), Zhou et al. proposed a Support Vector Machine (SVM) 
based system to extract syntactic and semantic relations. Kaushik and 
Chatterjee (2018) proposed a rule-based reasoning algorithm called 
RelExOnt that helps automatic relationship extraction from agricultural 
text. Zhang et al. (2019) proposed four types of deep learning models 
for relation extraction tasks that outperform the traditional machine 
learning models. Tymoshenko and Giuliano (2010) proposed a kernel-
based SVM method to extract semantic relations. Recent methods such 
as CNN (Alam et al., 2024), Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) (Zeng, 
Zhao, Li, & Dai, 2024), BERT (Han et al., 2024) and Quantum rein-
forcement learning (QRL) (Zhu, Mu, Wang, & Zhu, 2024) are being 
used to extract relations. Very few works have been done in the agri-
cultural domain with relation extraction. Qiao et al. (2021) proposed 
a BERT-BILSTM-LSTM model to detect entities and extract relations 
simultaneously. Further, the author created an agricultural knowledge 
graph. Zhang et al. (2022) proposed an NER model (BERT-BiLSTM-
CRF) and an RE model (PERT-FC) for the crop disease domain. They 
created a knowledge graph from the extracted entities and the extracted

4. Proposed methodology for NER and RE
In this section, we describe the methodology we followed to perform 
NER and RE on the AgriNER dataset to construct the agriculture knowl-
edge graph, which we named AgKG. A schematic representation of the 
complete methodology is shown in Fig.  1. More specifically, for NER 
and RE, we have fine-tuned RoBERTa-base models. In particular, we 
have added a shallow neural network classification head to a RoBERTa-
base model and fine-tuned the model. Thus, we have two models: one 
for NER, called AgNER-BERTa, as shown in Fig.  2, and another for RE, 
called AgRE-BERTa, as shown in Fig.  3.
4.1. Data augmentation
Deep learning models require a huge amount of data to train with. 
The AgriNER dataset is not large enough to fine-tune a huge deep neu-
ral model like RoBERTa. To overcome this problem, data augmentation 
can be used to increase the number of samples in the dataset. The com-
bination of the original data and the augmented data may contribute 
to better model training, and hence to the generalization performance 
of the model. So we decided to augment the training subset of AgriNER 
by generating synthetic examples that closely resemble the original 
examples. Our data augmentation method utilizes an LLM, specifically
GPT-3.5 Turbo, to generate augmented data separately for the NER 
and RE tasks. Our goal is data augmentation for NER is to generate 
synthetic examples similar to the examples available in the AgriNER 
dataset such that the entities are preserved in the synthetic examples 
while the surrounding context may be changed. In contrast, during data 
augmentation for RE, given a triplet containing a head entity, a relation, 
and a tail entity, a new triplet is generated where one or both entities 
are appropriately replaced but the relationship between the entity pair 
is same as in the original triplet.

4.2. Named entity recognition
Fig.  2 depicts the steps of building the trained AgNER-BERTa model, 
including the data augmentation, data validation, and model fine-
tuning steps. The architecture of RoBERTa (Liu et al., 2019) consists of 
a multi-layer bidirectional transformer encoder, including self-attention 
mechanisms, enabling the model to capture contextual information. 
Fine-tuning RoBERTa for specific tasks involves adding task-specific 
output layers while keeping the pre-trained transformer layers frozen. 
This enables efficient transfer learning and allows the model to adapt 
to various NLP applications. The RobertaForTokenClassifica-
tion from the Hugging Face Transformer Library6 is used for the NER 
task, which adds a linear token classification layer with softmax on 
top of RobertaModel. The model processes a sentence along with 
its BIO labels by providing input IDs, an attention mask, and the 
corresponding labels. During model inference, the AgNER-BERTa model 
takes new sentences, processes the input IDs, and generates contextual-
ized vector representations for each token in the input sequence. These 
representations capture the meaning of each token in the context of the 
entire sentence.
For example, given a sentence “ Nitrogen is essential for Rice.”, the 
AgNER-BERTa model predicts the BIO labels as: ⟨B-Nutrient, O, O, O, 
B-Crop, O⟩.

5. Experimental setup
In this section, we describe a performance study for our NER and RE 
models, AgNER-BERTa and AgRE-BERTa, respectively. All experiments 
were conducted on the same local device on an A4500 GPU.
We evaluate the performance of AgNER-BERTa and AgRE-BERTa
using several baseline models. In particular, we use the following 
encoder architectures: BERT,10 BERT-CRF (Souza, Nogueira, & Lotufo, 
2019), DitilBERT,11 RoBERTa, SciBERT,12 SpanBERT.13 We use de-
coder architectures that drive the popular ChatGPT tool, namely GPT-
3.5-Turbo and GPT-4o, as accessible via the OpenAI API.14 The BERT, 
DistilBERT, and RoBERTa are fine-tuned using the original dataset,
without the augmented data. The BERT-CRF (NER Only), SpanBERT, and 
SciBERT are fine-tuned using the augmented dataset to establish stronger 
baselines. The decoder models have been used in zero-shot settings 
where we use prompts to describe the tasks to the models. The prompts 
utilized are detailed in Fig.  A.13 in Appendix  A. Our models AgNER-
BERTa for the NER task and AgRE-BERTa for the RE task are based on 
RoBERTa, but they are trained on the augmented data.

5.3. Enhancement of AgriNER with data augmentation
We augment the AgriNER dataset (De et al., 2023) by applying data 
augmentation techniques that raise the size of the dataset and increase 
the frequency of entities and their relations. We have expanded the 
dataset not only to increase the number of entities and relations but also 
to create a domain-specific dataset that was previously lacking in this 
area. Tables  8 and 9 report the increased occurrences resulting from 
these data augmentation steps. The AgriNER dataset is created from 
180 agricultural research papers, with these data augmentation tech-
niques we have successfully included recent domain-specific knowledge 
to the dataset, which is not available in the dataset. Each augmented 
data is validated by both LLMs and humans to mitigate the possibility 
of wrong and false information.
6. Error analysis
Our models have performed exceedingly well overall, there are ar-
eas like the classification of rare entities and the extraction of ambigu-
ous relations where they went wrong. These mistakes have interesting 
implications for future improvements.
6.1. Data validation for NER
The model also performs very well on recent data, such as pre-
dicting NER and RE from recent unseen agricultural news, research 
papers, events, etc. Due to the ambiguousness of natural language, the 
model sometimes misclassified some rare entities. We have thoroughly 
analyzed the misclassified entities and relations to find a solution to 
overcome the issues. For example, consider the following sentence 𝑆
in an example 𝑒 in the test data:
Sentence ∶‘‘Huanglongbing is a bacterial infection that affects citrus
trees.’’
Tokens: ⟨Huanglongbing, is, a, bacterial, infection, that, affects, 
citrus, trees, .⟩
BIO Labels: ⟨B-Organism, O, O, B-Disease, I-Disease, O, O,
B-Crop, I-Crop, O⟩
As per our analysis, the error is likely due to the limited representation 
of the “ Huanglongbing” entity in the raw training data (De et al., 2023). 
It occurs only 9 times in the augmented dataset, this small sample 
size made it difficult for the model to learn a robust representation of 
this entity type, particularly given its semantic similarity to bacterial 
organisms. Out of the 9 occurrences, it occurs 4 times in the test 
data, resulting in correct classification as “ Disease” in 2 cases and 
misclassification as “ Organism” in the remaining 2 cases, thus attaining 
approximately 67% Precision (𝑃 ) and 50% Recall (𝑅). For a different

• Original Sentence: “ Nitrogen plays a crucial role in agriculture

discarded data. We have manually verified 1500 generated sentences 
and found that only 15.6% (234) of them were semantically dissimilar 
or did not match the entity types. As a result, only 1266 sentences were 
further validated. This indicates that the generator model sometimes 
hallucinates and therefore, data validation is required.

6.2. Data validation for RE
Generation and validation of the dataset are very time and resource-
intensive. We have designed the data augmentation methods to guar-
antee the successful generation and validation of sentences within the 
dataset (De et al., 2023). Due to hallucinations, an LLM can create a 
triplet that falsely associates a crop with a disease that it does not nor-
mally relate to. While the designed data augmentation methods ensured 
the successful generation and validation of sentences, limitations arose 
due to hallucinations in the LLMs. For example, consider a sentence 𝑠
in the RE data validation:
Sentence ∶‘‘The fungus Apiosporina morbosa is causing a black knot
plant disease.’’
Relatios: ⟨Apiosporina morbosa, Caused_By, Black knot⟩
As described in the AgriNER dataset (De et al., 2023), “ Caused_By” 
is an asymmetric relation, hence the above example does not satisfy the 
relation between the entities (Black knot is not caused by the fungus 
Apiosporina morbosa). Out of 4891 synthetically generated relations, 
the validation system flagged 660 (13.5%) relations as incorrect. These 
errors primarily involved semantically incompatible entities. We have 
also found that, while generating and predicting the classes of named 
entities or relations, the GPT-based models created many new classes 
that the dataset does not support even though we specified the exact 
set of classes in the prompt. This is an instance of hallucination, which 
is a major limitation of LLMs. For example, the LLM generated overly 
generic relations, which are also absent in the relation set of our

knowledge graphs in agriculture.
7. Encoder or decoder: Which is a better model for NER and RE?
NER is a token classification task that assigns a classification label to 
each token in the input sequence. Encoder-based architecture provides 
a rich contextual embedding for each token in a sequence, capturing 
both the left and right context. In contrast, decoder-based architectures 
are primarily suitable for generative tasks. They work unidirectionally, 
predicting the next token in a sequence given the previous ones. This 
makes them well-suited for tasks where the model needs to generate 
text. Relation Extraction is also a classification task. Encoder-based 
models like RoBERTa process the entire input sequence at once and are 
typically used for tasks requiring an understanding of the full context, 
such as classification or extraction tasks.
In our work, the RoBERTa model is used as the leading encoder-
based architecture, and GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4o models are used as 
the representative decoder-based architectures. Our experiments show 
that encoder-based models are good at understanding the context in 
NER and RE tasks (as shown in Table  6 and Fig.  B.14), where the mean-
ing and classification of a word can depend on the context formed by 
the surrounding words. Additionally, since the architecture and weights 
of RoBERTa are publicly available, it can be fine-tuned for NER and 
RE tasks, thus allowing it to utilize both its pre-trained knowledge and 
the added domain knowledge. We have observed that the performance 
of GPT in these tasks on the given dataset is lower. Though some 
researchers have made attempts to build NER and RE systems with 
GPT (Wan et al., 2023; Wang, Sun et al., 2023), the decoder model does 
not appear to be a natural fit for this problem. Moreover, while GPT and 
many other LLMs are closed-source, even open-source LLMs are often so 
large that training them effectively becomes a highly computationally 
intensive task. Another problem with LLMs is that they hallucinate. So, 
even when they are prompted to identify entities and relations from a 
given pre-defined set, they may generate entity types and relations from 
outside this set. Therefore, we believe encoder-based architectures are 
more suitable for NER and RE tasks.
8. Future work
Future developments of NER and RE models for the agricultural 
domain will find interesting opportunities to enhance information ex-
traction. The expansion of the dataset can hugely improve the precision 
of the model to make it comprise more varied and rare entities, such 
as specific crops and diseases. Adding the aspect of multilingualism 
to these models is another important step because it will make it 
possible for the researchers to process agriculture data from regions 
worldwide. Domain-specific language models fine-tuned on large cor-
pora of agricultural texts can also be used to improve quality in 
entity and relation extraction. Data augmentation technologies, such as 
adversarial methods or back-translation, will further enrich the training 
set with diverse data to improve model performance. Error correction 
mechanisms introducing rule-based systems or ensemble learning will 
minimize misclassification. Such factors could transform agricultural 
decision-making based on dynamic and data-driven solutions for crop 
management and resource optimization, in addition to building climate 
resilience through integrating real-time data sources with IoT sensors 
and satellite imagery.
9. Conclusion
This research shows that the NER and RE systems can effectively 
be used for better information extraction from agricultural research 
publications, a low-resource domain. We incorporate domain-specific 
LLM-based data augmentation strategies which are validated by both 
the LLMs and human reviewers to enhance the performance of these 
models in a low-resource setting. We further demonstrate the effec-
tiveness and robustness of our approach with state-of-the-art baseline 
comparisons, including SciBERT and SpanBERT.
One key contribution of the work is the construction of the AgKG, 
an amalgamation of the extracted entities and their relationships orga-
nized into a structured and queryable format. The AgKG has practical 
applications in agricultural decision-making, such as optimizing crop 
management, monitoring pest outbreaks, and supporting sustainable 
farming practices. The scalability of the AgKG and our AgNER-BERTa 
and AgRE-BERTa model makes it a valuable resource for researchers, 
policymakers, and practitioners and contributes to advancements in 
precision agriculture.
In conclusion, this work demonstrates the potential of combining 
fine-tuned encoder-based models with augmented datasets to tackle 
domain-specific challenges in agriculture. Future research will focus 
on expanding the scope of the AgKG and validating the models on 
additional datasets to enhance their generalizability and societal impact 
further. We hope that our contributions will lead to better knowl-
edge representation and decision-making using such knowledge graphs 
despite the limitations of low-resource domains.
